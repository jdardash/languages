# Tagalog

**The book:** *Tagalog for Beginners* — Joi Barrios (Tuttle).
The most complete modern self-study textbook for Tagalog (a thin field): dialogues,
grammar, audio, cultural notes, to a conversational base. Classic runner-up: *Basic
Tagalog* — Paraluman Aspillera (revised ed.), tighter on grammar drills.

**Free and legal alternative:** FSI Tagalog and Peace Corps Tagalog materials
(public domain): https://www.livelingua.com

**First move:** Barrios lesson 1; front-load the focus/trigger system (the verb
system unlike anything in European languages) with patience — it clicks late.

## Path to fluency (learn everything here → B2/C1)

1. **Foundation:** Tagalog for Beginners, then *Intermediate Tagalog* (Barrios's
   own continuation, Tuttle) — together the most complete printed path that exists
   for this language.
2. **The workhorse site:** Tagalog.com — dictionary, drills, reader, audio corpus;
   the best single online resource: https://www.tagalog.com
3. **Input engine:**
   - Filipino YouTube vlogs and teleseryes (ABS-CBN/GMA clips are abundant and
     free) — real-world Tagalog is Taglish, which makes native media friendlier
     than the textbook suggests.
   - Filipino films and series on Netflix with subtitles.
4. **Speaking:** tutor on https://www.italki.com — Tagalog's difficulty is almost
   entirely the verb focus system, and it only automatizes through conversation
   volume, not grammar study.
5. **Native transition:** sustained conversation with native speakers; code-switch
   freely (natives do) and let the Tagalog share grow.

**Milestone that means "fluent":** you follow a teleserye episode without subtitles
and hold an hour of mixed Taglish conversation, producing correct focus forms
without thinking about them.
