# Japanese Learning Research — method, dose, and honest costs

Deep-research run 2026-07-17, same verified pipeline as the chess report (claims
adversarially checked, 3 votes each). Key honesty note: **no hour-count timeline
(including the famous FSI ~2,200-hour figure) survived verification**, and no specific
resource comparison (Genki vs. Bunpro vs. WaniKani) was verified head-to-head. What
did survive is the method structure and — the most useful finding — the economics of
maintenance.

## The strongest finding: push high, then maintain cheaply (high confidence)

Peer-reviewed 8-year longitudinal data (Tracy-Ventura et al. 2025, *Language Learning*
75(1), open access): advanced instructed L2 learners showed **very little attrition
over 6 years post-instruction even with only limited continued exposure**. Oral
proficiency, lexical diversity, and vocabulary all held; the *only* significant decline
for low-exposure learners was speech-rate fluency. The gap versus consistent users came
from the consistent group *continuing to improve*, not from the limited group losing
skill. And **higher peak attainment predicted less attrition** (interaction beta =
-0.44, p = .027), converging with the older critical-threshold literature (Bahrick 1984).

Caveat: n=28, French/Spanish (Category I), advanced learners — extending this to
intermediate Japanese (kanji may decay differently than oral skill) is an argued
inference, not a finding.

**Implication for the portfolio:** the expensive thing is being *mid-acquisition* in
many languages at once. Skills parked at a high level are cheap to keep; skills parked
at a low level evaporate. So: finish the Russian push first, keep Japanese at
guilt-free dabble until a real slot opens, then — if promoted — push it hard to a
durable level rather than simmering at 15 min/day forever.

## Method structure (verified as what Refold prescribes, not proof it beats alternatives)

Refold's roadmap (verified live 2026-07-17):

- **Input-first, delayed output.** Build reading-with-audio comprehension, then
  natural-speed listening; speaking is Phase 4, permitted early but not recommended.
- **Kanji through vocabulary, not isolated character study.** Words and kanji learning
  become synonymous; RTK/KKLC is acknowledged only as an optional path, never a
  prerequisite. No controlled head-to-head evidence exists that either RTK-first or
  vocab-first wins — but the vocab-first route avoids RTK's known structural drawback
  (keywords without readings; you can "know" 2,200 characters and read nothing).

Community critiques worth keeping in mind (extracted, lower tier): pure
"follow your interests" immersion can produce domain-hopping — strong anime
vocabulary, weak everyday vocabulary; and one documented self-learner at ~15-20
hrs/week (WaniKani + Anki + Bunpro + Genki + heavy anime) reached ~4,000 SRS words and
800-900 kanji in a year yet only upper-beginner (N5-ish) level — a sobering calibration
for what "a year of serious effort" buys in a Category V language.

## Practical shape when Japanese gets a slot

- **Dabble mode (now):** kana + a starter SRS deck (kaishi-style core vocab) and
  curiosity-driven input. 10-15 min/day ceiling. Goal is familiarity, not progress —
  do not open a second front against Russian.
- **Project mode (if ever promoted):** SRS vocab (WaniKani or Anki core deck) + one
  grammar spine (Genki or Bunpro — unverified which is better; pick one, finish it) +
  daily reading-with-audio input, scaling toward graded readers and native content
  with lookup tools. Output (italki tutoring) once comprehension is real. Expect the
  14-20 month B1 estimate in `../../README.md` to be optimistic at low weekly hours.
- **Time-wasters flagged in sources (unverified tier, but consistent):** Duolingo as a
  primary tool, RTK as a months-long prerequisite before touching real Japanese,
  resource-collecting and app-switching instead of one spine plus input volume.

## Open questions

- Do the cheap-maintenance findings generalize from Category I languages to kanji
  (orthography may decay differently)?
- RTK vs. vocab-in-context: no controlled comparison exists.
- True dose-response for hours/week to JLPT/CEFR level.
