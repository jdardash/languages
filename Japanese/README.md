# Japanese

**The book:** *Genki I* (3rd ed.) — Banno, Ikeda, Ohno, Shinagawa, Tokashiki.
The overwhelming consensus first textbook: kana, core grammar, dialogues, workbook.
Finish both Genki volumes with understanding and you hold a genuine N4 base.

**Free and legal alternatives:** Tae Kim's *Guide to Japanese Grammar*
(https://guidetojapanese.org — the standard free grammar spine); *Irodori* (Japan
Foundation's official free course): https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp

**First move:** hiragana and katakana to automaticity (1-2 weeks), then Genki
lesson 1 with vocabulary in Anki.

## Path to fluency (learn everything here → B2/C1, ~JLPT N2)

1. **Foundation:** Genki I → Genki II (→ N4), then *Quartet I & II* (same publisher,
   the standard intermediate continuation → N3-N2 grammar). Tobira is the
   alternative intermediate spine.
2. **Kanji/vocab engine (parallel, from day one):** Anki — vocab-first kanji in
   context, per the method already researched in `Japanese Learning Research.md`
   (not months of RTK). WaniKani (https://www.wanikani.com) if you want it
   pre-structured.
3. **Reading engine:**
   - Tadoku free graded readers (start immediately, level 0):
     https://tadoku.org/japanese/en/free-books-en
   - Satori Reader — graded stories with audio, from upper-beginner:
     https://www.satorireader.com
4. **Listening engine:**
   - Nihongo con Teppei — the standard learner podcast: https://nihongoconteppei.com
   - Comprehensible Japanese (graded video): https://cijapanese.com
5. **Speaking:** tutor on https://www.italki.com from Genki II onward; HelloTalk for
   daily written exchange.
6. **Native transition:** anime/drama with Japanese subtitles, then without; novels
   (Kiki's Delivery Service is the classic first native novel).

**Milestone that means "fluent":** JLPT N2 passable; you follow a natural-speed
conversation among Japanese speakers and participate without translating in your
head. FSI Category V — with Chinese and Arabic, the longest road here.
