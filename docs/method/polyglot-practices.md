# What Top Language Learners Actually Do — Practitioner Survey Cross-Checked Against SLA Evidence

Researched 2026-08-09. Companion to `2026-08-08 - Language Learning Research.md`
(the meta-analytic evidence layer). This file covers the practitioner side: ten
accomplished learners/programs, what they concretely do, whether it survives
contact with the evidence, and where the monetization bias sits.
Effect sizes referenced below are from the 2026-08-08 file (explicit instruction
g≈1.06; interaction/corrective feedback d≈1.09/0.83; spaced retrieval; extensive
reading d=0.46; captions; HVPT g≈0.92; Nation coverage thresholds).

## 1. Luca Lampariello — Bidirectional Translation (BDT)

**Practice.** A 6+ day cycle on short bilingual texts (100–500 words; Assimil
dialogues are his canonical material): Day 1 — listen while reading L2 text
against the L1 translation until total comprehension; later sessions — translate
L2→L1 in writing; days later — back-translate your L1 version into L2 from
memory and compare word-by-word against the original, analyzing every
divergence. Repeat every 3–5 days; 30–60 min daily, "frequency beats intensity"
([lucalampariello.com/translation-learn-any-language](https://www.lucalampariello.com/translation-learn-any-language/),
[forum.language-learners.org thread](https://forum.language-learners.org/viewtopic.php?t=19905),
[mondecast.com](https://mondecast.com/the-bi-directional-translation-method/)).
He documented doing one Assimil lesson daily for 30+ consecutive days
([Facebook post](https://m.facebook.com/LucaLamparielloCoaching/photos/a.3331286153551185/5169165819763200/?type=3)).

**Claims.** "Well on your way to intermediate in 3–6 months" — this is his paid
course's sales page ([lucalampariello.com/master-language-learner](https://www.lucalampariello.com/master-language-learner/)).

**Evidence fit.** Strong. Back-translation with comparison is effectively
retrieval practice plus focus-on-form plus immediate corrective feedback (the
original text is the feedback) — consistent with explicit instruction (g≈1.06)
and feedback effects. The delayed back-translation step is spaced retrieval
(Kim & Webb). Contradicts nothing in the meta-analyses; contradicts input-purist
dogma against translation.

**Bias check.** The technique itself is fully described free in blog posts; the
paid course sells packaging and coaching. Timeline claims are marketing-soft but
not outrageous.

## 2. Steve Kaufmann — Massive Input / LingQ

**Practice.** Listening threaded through daily life (breakfast, workouts, car —
1+ hr/day), plus reading with instant dictionary lookup in LingQ; mini-stories
re-listened dozens of times; deliberately delays speaking until substantial
comprehension exists
([lingq.com method page](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-languages-like-steve-kaufmann/),
[Kaufmann on Medium](https://medium.com/@lingosteve/effective-language-learning-listening-and-reading-e82a0afaa020),
[StoryLearning profile](https://storylearning.com/blog/steve-kaufmann)).

**Claims.** ~1 hr/day → one proficiency level per 90 days; 20 languages over a
lifetime (most learned in his 60s–70s, several to modest conversational levels —
his advanced languages took years).

**Evidence fit.** Aligned with extensive reading (d=0.46 — real but moderate)
and Nation's coverage logic (massive input is the only way to meet low-frequency
words enough times). Where he diverges from evidence: he is dismissive of
drills, grammar study, and error correction, yet explicit instruction and
corrective feedback carry the *largest* effect sizes in the set. His approach
works but is plausibly slower per hour than input+explicit hybrid.

**Bias check.** Significant: Kaufmann co-founded and owns LingQ, a subscription
product whose design (reading with lookups, word counts) *is* the method. His
anti-grammar rhetoric conveniently matches what his tool sells.

## 3. Alexander Argüelles — Shadowing and Scriptorium

**Practice.** Shadowing: repeat native audio verbatim, near-simultaneously,
while walking briskly outdoors, perfectly upright, articulating loudly and
clearly; progresses from "blind" shadowing to shadowing with L1/L2 texts in
hand (Assimil-style bilingual courses)
([learnanylanguage.fandom.com/wiki/Shadowing](https://learnanylanguage.fandom.com/wiki/Shadowing),
[technique summary gist](https://gist.github.com/jhwheeler/2829520a5f4b330dce8177ce9e53415e),
[mezzoguild.com](https://www.mezzoguild.com/language-shadowing-a-superior-learning-method/)).
Scriptorium: read a sentence aloud, say each word aloud while writing it, read
the written sentence aloud; look up everything unknown — deliberate
slowing-down for form ([same gist](https://gist.github.com/jhwheeler/2829520a5f4b330dce8177ce9e53415e)).
His self-documented study logs (language-learners forum era) show extreme
volume — many hours daily across dozens of languages.

**Claims.** Reading knowledge of dozens of languages; he is an academic, makes
few speed claims, and his spoken fluency in some claimed languages has been
questioned ([critical review](https://ouicommunicate.com/is-alexander-arguelles-legit-a-critical-look-at-his-french-and-his-methods/)).

**Evidence fit.** Shadowing is essentially high-intensity phonological output
practice with an immediate model — mechanistically adjacent to HVPT-style
perception–production training (g≈0.92) and to the Mikel/Hyperlingua shadowing
step. Scriptorium is multi-modal explicit form-focus. The walking/posture claims
have no experimental support — that part is ritual. Aligned overall; his
portfolio skews receptive, matching an input-heavy, interaction-light regimen
(interaction d=1.09 is what he under-uses).

**Bias check.** Low. He monetizes little; his weakness is volume-as-method
(unreplicable hours) rather than selling.

## 4. Kató Lomb — Reading-First

**Practice.** From *Polyglot: How I Learn Languages*: buy a pulp novel in the
target language and read it with a pen, guessing from context ("base your
progress on the known, not the unknown"); look up a word only if it recurs and
still blocks meaning; record words *with their context*, never isolated;
supplement with radio, and a daily minimum "10-minute monologue" (self-talk);
switch activity rather than quit when bored
([TESL-EJ book](https://african.wisc.edu/content/kat%C3%B3-lomb-polyglot-how-i-learn-languages-berkeley-tesl-ej-2008),
[Ten Commandments](https://interpretertrainingresources.eu/language/10-commandments-lomb/),
[StoryLearning summary](https://storylearning.com/kato-lomb),
[review](https://autolingual.com/kato-lomb-polyglot-review/)).
Her formula: results = time invested × motivation ÷ inhibition.

**Claims.** 16 working languages; earned money in ~10 as
interpreter/translator. Pre-internet, no product, self-reported.

**Evidence fit.** Extensive reading (d=0.46), context-based vocabulary
(consistent with contextualized learning findings), daily spaced contact. Her
tolerance of ambiguity anticipates coverage-threshold research, though modern
evidence suggests she'd have progressed faster pairing reading with deliberate
retrieval — which her context-notebook partially was. No conflicts.

**Bias check.** None. Dead, sold nothing. The most credible self-report in the
genre, but n=1 with unusual motivation.

## 5. Matt vs Japan / Refold — Immersion + Sentence Mining

**Practice.** Four-stage roadmap: (1) phonetics/script + core frequency vocab
via Anki; (2) massive input (target ~1+ hr active immersion/day, more passive)
with continuous *sentence mining* — pulling "one-target" sentences (one unknown
element) from your own immersion into Anki (~10 new cards/day typical
guidance); structured *monolingual transition* to target-language dictionaries;
(3) output begins only after strong comprehension, via imitation and
conversation; (4) specialization ([refold.la/roadmap](https://refold.la/roadmap/),
[monolingual transition page](https://refold.la/roadmap/stage-2/c/structured-monolingual-transition/)).
Matt himself did a multi-year AJATT regimen (all-day immersion, at peak brutal
Anki loads) to near-native Japanese ([StoryLearning history](https://storylearning.com/blog/matt-vs-japan)).

**Claims.** Near-native possible in ~4–5 years of extreme immersion. Notably,
Matt later *repudiated* parts of his own method: influenced by ALG, he now
argues conscious study caused "interference" with his pitch accent and
pronunciation ([Migaku interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6j4AOsEFug),
[I'm Learning Mandarin podcast](https://imlearningmandarin.com/2022/04/28/matt-vs-japan-on-input-stephen-krashen-why-accents-matter-much-more-podcast/)).
Treat his current interference doctrine skeptically — it contradicts the
explicit-instruction and HVPT evidence, and coincides with launching a new paid
community ([Immersion Dojo on Skool](https://www.skool.com/mattvsjapan/about));
community controversies also exist ([WaniKani thread](https://community.wanikani.com/t/beware-of-matt-vs-japan/55261)).

**Evidence fit.** Sentence mining + Anki = spaced retrieval in context
(strongly supported); massive input = coverage logic; early phonetics =
HVPT-compatible. The *delayed output* stance conflicts with interaction/CF
effects (d=1.09/0.83). Verdict: the Stage 1–2 toolkit is the most
evidence-aligned part; the output-delay ideology is the least.

**Bias check.** Refold sells decks, courses, community tiers; the roadmap
itself is free and concrete.

## 6. Pablo Román / Dreaming Spanish — Comprehensible-Input Purism

**Practice.** Watch graded comprehensible-input video only: Superbeginner
(0–50 hrs, slow, visual) through Advanced across a 7-level, ~1500-hour roadmap;
explicit *prohibitions* — no English, no grammar study, no flashcards, no
subtitles, no early speaking. Speaking recommended from Level 6 (~1000 hrs),
optionally Level 5 (~600 hrs) ([dreaming.com/faq](https://www.dreaming.com/faq),
[roadmap PDF](https://www.scribd.com/document/793209760/Language-Learning-Roadmap-by-Dreaming-Spanish),
[review](https://palteca.com/dreaming-spanish-review)).

**Claims.** The roadmap warns that speaking before ~600 hours "will invariably
result in hard-to-fix non-native pronunciation, noticeably bad grammar, and
poor word usage."

**Evidence fit.** The input core is supported (CI drives acquisition; the
graded-video library is a genuinely excellent implementation). The prohibitions
are where it breaks with evidence: "invariably" fossilized pronunciation from
early speaking has no meta-analytic support; banning flashcards discards spaced
retrieval; banning grammar discards g≈1.06; banning captions/subtitles
contradicts Montero Perez. This is Krashen/ALG doctrine hardened past what data
show.

**Bias check.** Moderate: subscription platform whose value proposition *is*
"you need 1500 hours of our videos"; the hour targets and speaking prohibitions
lengthen the subscription. The hour milestones derive from AUA Thai anecdata,
not controlled study.

## 7. Gabriel Wyner — Fluent Forever

**Practice.** Order of operations: (1) train ear first with minimal-pair
discrimination tests + IPA (this *is* HVPT, commercialized); (2) learn the 625
most common concrete words via self-made Anki cards using personal images and
native audio (Forvo), never translations; (3) grammar via example sentences on
cards; 15–30 new cards/day, ~30 min/day review
([book notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/fluent-forever-gabriel-wyner),
[reader review](https://www.julian.ac/blog/2014/08/30/reading-fluent-forever/),
[results review](https://minimalist.travel/learn/fluent-forever-review/)).

**Claims.** Book recounts advanced French in ~5 months intensive; the app
markets "fluency in six months" ([app review](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/fluent-forever/)).
Wyner had unusual advantages (opera singer, prior IPA mastery, full-time study).

**Evidence fit.** The strongest laboratory pedigree of any guru: minimal pairs
= HVPT (g≈0.92); self-made image cards = spaced retrieval + generation effect +
dual coding; the "no translation" rule is his weakest plank — SLA evidence does
not show L1 translations harm vocabulary learning (initial L1 links are often
*more* efficient); it mainly serves product differentiation. Method is also
front-loaded on isolated words, light on input volume and interaction.

**Bias check.** High on the app ($9.99+/mo, mixed reviews, weaker than the
book's DIY prescription); the book itself is honest about workload.

## 8. Scott Young & Vat Jaiswal — The Year Without English

**Practice.** 2013–14: four countries × ~3 months (Spain, Brazil, China,
Korea); rule: no English from day one, including with each other (exceptions:
work, family, <1 hr/week) — plus conventional tools (prior prep, tutors,
flashcards) underneath the constraint
([project page](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/),
[announcement](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2013/08/25/the-year-without-english/)).

**Results (his own retrospective).** Conversational Spanish/Portuguese/Chinese
(HSK4 in ~3 months), near-conversational Korean; admits they "technically
failed" the rule, that Asia was much harder, and that zero-prep immersion "is
usually not going to work"
([tywe-review](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2014/09/01/tywe-review/),
[interview](https://www.mezzoguild.com/scott-h-young-year-without-english/)).

**Evidence fit.** The constraint maximizes interaction + implicit corrective
feedback (d=1.09/0.83 — the biggest effects in the set) and forced output. His
Korea failure is a clean natural experiment: interaction without sufficient
comprehensible input/prior explicit knowledge stalls — evidence for the hybrid
view.

**Bias check.** Low-moderate: content marketing for his Ultralearning brand,
but unusually honest about failures.

## 9. Coaches: Machová and Simcott

**Machová** (9 languages, never lived abroad; interviewed/observed hundreds of
polyglots) found *no shared method* — Benny Lewis speaks from day one from
phrasebooks; "Lucas" learned Russian by copying strangers' Skype chats; others
start with frequency lists or grammar. What all share: (1) they *enjoy* the
process (self-chosen materials — Harry Potter, Friends); (2) effective memory
methods (she names Anki, Memrise, Goldlist — i.e., spaced repetition); (3) a
*system*: fixed daily slots, recurring in dead time (15 min before breakfast,
podcast commutes); (4) patience — visible progress in ~2 months, fluency in
years ([TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/lydia_machova_the_secrets_of_learning_a_new_language),
[transcript](https://singjupost.com/the-secrets-of-learning-a-new-language-lydia-machova-transcript/)).
She sells mentoring ([languagementoring.com](https://www.languagementoring.com/lydia-machova/))
but her talk sells no method — which is precisely its finding.

**Simcott** (~50 languages studied, professional consultant; co-founder of the
Polyglot Conference): daily "minimum effective dose," constant conscious
engagement ("thinking about the language consciously is THE most important
thing"), explicitly deflates speed promises — "there's no magic pill"
([Language Mastery podcast](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/language-mastery/episodes/Hyperpolyglot-Richard-Simcott-on-How-to-Learn--Retain-Multiple-Languages-e18i5b0),
[italki interview](https://www.italki.com/en/article/1148/interview-richard-simcott)).

## 10. FSI/DLI — The Professional Benchmark

**Practice.** Small classes (≤6), ~23 classroom hrs + ~17 self-study hrs per
week (a 40-hour job); 50-minute blocks; mornings mechanical (mimicry,
pattern/substitution drills — the audiolingual inheritance), afternoons
communicative (role-play, reading, lab), per Stevick's "microwave cycle" of
tight drill-to-use intervals; heavy corrective feedback throughout
([FSI method breakdown](https://preplearnio.com/method/),
[Georgetown GURT paper on FSI pedagogy](http://sealang.net/archives/sla/gurt_1999_07.pdf),
[category hours](https://www.lingobright.com/statistics/fsi-language-difficulty-rankings-and-hours-to-fluency/)).

**Results.** The only *institutionally verified* timelines in this list:
professional working proficiency (ILR 3) in ~600–750 hrs for Category I
languages up to ~2200 hrs for Category IV — with selected, full-time,
career-motivated learners.

**Evidence fit.** FSI is basically the meta-analyses operationalized: explicit
instruction + drills to automaticity + intensive interaction + feedback +
volume. Its persistence with pattern drill despite audiolingualism's academic
fall suggests drills work *as one layer*, not as the whole method. No
monetization bias — it publishes its hour requirements as costs, not promises.

## Synthesis: Convergence, Disagreement, Verdicts

**Everyone converges on five things**, whatever the branding:

1. **Daily contact, engineered into the day** (Lomb's 10-minute floor,
   Kaufmann's breakfast listening, Machová's system principle, Lampariello's
   chain, FSI's schedule). Frequency beats session length.
2. **Massive comprehensible input** as the calorie base — universal from Lomb
   to FSI's self-study hours.
3. **Some spaced memory system** — Anki (Refold, Wyner, Machová's polyglots),
   LingQ's lingQs, Lomb's context notebook, BDT's 3–5-day cycles, Mikel's
   active recall. Dreaming Spanish is the *only* holdout.
4. **Self-chosen, personally interesting material** — the motivational
   load-bearing wall (Machová's core finding).
5. **Honest hour counts.** Every verified result (FSI, Young's HSK4, Matt's
   years, Dreaming Spanish's 1500 hrs) lands in the hundreds-to-thousands of
   hours; only the marketing copy says months.

**The three real disputes, and where evidence points:**

- **Early speaking vs silent period.** Purists (Dreaming Spanish, late-Matt)
  vs day-one speakers (Benny Lewis, Young, FSI). Evidence: interaction and
  corrective feedback carry d≈0.83–1.09 — the largest effects available; no
  meta-analytic support for fossilization-from-early-output. Verdict: early-ish
  output with feedback wins, atop an input base (Young's Korea failure shows
  output without input also stalls).
- **Grammar/explicit study vs pure input.** Kaufmann/Román vs
  Lampariello/Wyner/FSI. Evidence: explicit instruction g≈1.06. Verdict:
  hybrid. Pure input works but is the slow path per hour; pure study without
  input fails on coverage grounds.
- **Translation: tool or poison.** Wyner and Refold ban it; Lampariello builds
  his whole method on it; Lomb used bilingual lookups freely. Evidence: no
  meta-analytic support for the ban; back-translation functions as retrieval +
  feedback. Verdict: translation as an *exercise* is defensible; the ban is
  branding.

**Bias pattern:** the strength of a guru's prohibitions correlates with what
their product replaces (LingQ ↔ anti-grammar; Dreaming Spanish subscription ↔
anti-everything-else; Fluent Forever app ↔ anti-translation). The least
monetized voices (Lomb, Argüelles, FSI, Machová's meta-observation) make the
fewest exclusive claims.

## Implementable Practices for a Personal Web App (used by top performers AND evidence-supported)

1. **Minimal-pair ear-training drill** (Wyner ⇄ HVPT g≈0.92): audio A/B forced
   choice on target-language contrasts, multiple speakers, immediate feedback,
   run in the first weeks and interleaved forever.
2. **Sentence cards mined from your own input, one unknown per card**
   (Refold/Mikel ⇄ spaced retrieval, contextualized vocab): card front = audio
   + sentence with gap; back = answer + image; FSRS/SM-2 scheduling; cap
   ~10–20 new/day.
3. **Bidirectional translation cycle as a card type** (Lampariello ⇄ retrieval
   + explicit form-focus + self-feedback): store short passages; Day 0
   comprehension pass, Day 2 L2→L1, Day 5 L1→L2 from your own translation,
   then machine-diff against the original and highlight divergences.
4. **Shadowing player** (Argüelles/Mikel/FSI mimicry ⇄ output automatization):
   sentence-level audio loop with 0.7–1.0x speed, record-and-compare, optional
   transcript toggle (blind → text-supported), TTS generation from the user's
   own sentence bank.
5. **Captioned video/audio input log with hour counter** (Kaufmann/Dreaming
   Spanish ⇄ extensive input + captions meta-analysis): track cumulative
   comprehensible-input hours against FSI-category-calibrated milestones, with
   target-language captions on by default.
6. **Context notebook, not word list** (Lomb ⇄ Nation): every saved word stores
   its source sentence; re-encounter counts drive review priority.
7. **Daily minimum-dose streak with activity switching**
   (Lomb/Machová/Simcott): a 10–15 minute floor, and when the user stalls on
   one activity the app offers a different modality instead of ending the
   session.
8. **Timed micro-output with feedback** (FSI/Young ⇄ interaction d=1.09):
   daily 2-minute recorded monologue or pattern-substitution drill ("same
   sentence, new subject/tense"), with LLM corrective feedback in recast form —
   the app's substitute for a tutor.
9. **Honest dashboard**: display total hours against evidence-based ranges
   (600–2200 FSI hours), never "fluent in 3 months."
