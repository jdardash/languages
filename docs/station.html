01 — Reconciliation

The two day-plans in your repo, merged

METHOD.md gives a day that is 50% recall on your own sentences, 30% grammar book, 20% input. How To Learn Languages.md gives a different day: grammar spine, captioned video, a frequency deck, two tutor sessions a week, minimal-pair drills. Each file calls itself “the day.” Neither contains the other’s largest component — there is no SRS line in the first and no sentence-recall line in the second. Across the two files and the README there are four different figures for how long a day takes: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 45–60 minutes, and a routine table totalling up to three hours.

The military schools resolved this a long time ago, and not by choosing. A day at the Defense Language Institute is roughly six classroom hours that move through several different activities with different purposes — and the thing that makes it one day rather than a pile of tactics is that each block ends in something you could not do before. Your two files are not rival philosophies. One is a production engine and one is a comprehension engine, and you need both, in that ratio, in that order.

The one thing to fix in the tooling. How To Learn Languages.md overturns METHOD.md on pronunciation and prescribes generating sentence audio in several different voices, because the number of distinct talkers is what makes perception training work. But build_audio.py takes a single --voice, and its cache key is built from that one voice string, so multi-voice output cannot currently be expressed. The correction is written down and unimplemented.
And one number that does not add up. recall_drill.py caps its Leitner ladder at 14 days, so a fully-mature sentence returns every fortnight forever. The SRS arithmetic in your own notes assumes mature cards sit at 60–150 days. At a 14-day ceiling a thousand-sentence list costs about 71 reviews a day indefinitely — roughly five times what you budgeted. The drill below uses the longer ladder.

02 — Drill floor

Say it out loud. Thinking it does not count.

All 32 sentences from Sentence Islands/Spanish/sentences.csv, live. Progress saves in this browser. Four modes, each borrowed from a different part of the schoolhouse day — recall is your own method, the other three are the ones nobody self-studying ever trains.

Ready

Press start.

Space reveals the answer. 1 missed it, 2 had it, R replays audio.

Recall

The engine. Getting it wrong and then seeing the answer is what builds the memory — a re-read of the answer you never tried to produce does almost nothing.

Shadow

Speak simultaneously with the audio, not after it. Builds mouth timing at full speed. The thinnest evidence base of anything here — no meta-analysis exists.

Gist

One pass, no replay, then say what it was about in English. This is the actual intelligence-linguist skill, and it is the one self-studiers never practise.

03 — Technique library

What transfers to thirty minutes alone

Two facts frame everything below. The first is that the Foreign Service Institute measured this in 1967 and found a student working alone with recorded material participates with the language at 400–600 times an hour, against 100–150 in a class shared with three other people. For the drilling layer, working alone is denser, not degraded.

The second is that the Army's own sustainment requirement for a qualified linguist is 8–16 hours a month plus one annual block of 160 consecutive hours, against a written individual plan, with a real test at the end. That is the military's own answer to “what keeps a professional at level,” and unlike sixty weeks of full-time school, it is a dose you can actually hit.

One caution before you start collecting. The drills people associate with the “military method” are a 1940s inheritance the institution has explicitly moved past — its current catalogue describes task-based, content-based, learner-centered instruction, and pattern drills appear nowhere in it. Its instructor certification course does not teach them. What is worth taking is the architecture — short cycles that end in real use, authentic material from day one, diagnose-then-target — at least as much as the drills.

04 — The ladder

Where the honest benchmark actually sits

The Interagency Language Roundtable scale is the one the US government actually pays against, and it is worth internalising because it is far less flattering than the scales used in language marketing. Level 2 is called limited working proficiency. It is where most graduates of a full-time, sixty-week, selectively-admitted military course land.

0+
Memorised

Fixed phrases. Cannot recombine. Most app streaks live here.

1
Elementary

Survives predictable everyday exchanges. Frequent errors, constant repetition needed.

Handles routine work demands and concrete topics. Still breaks down on abstraction, argument and unfamiliar register. This is the military graduation target — and roughly 3 in 10 people who start the course do not reach it.

CEFR B2ACTFL AdvancedThe real bar
2+

Only about a quarter to a third of graduating classes reach this in listening and reading together.

Abstract topics, argument, unfamiliar subject matter, structural accuracy that never impedes. Government auditors put linguist productivity at up to four times higher here than at level 2. This is what people mean when they say “fluent.”

CEFR C1ACTFL Superior
4–5
Advanced professional → native

Level 5 is an articulate educated native speaker. It is not a study goal.

Read pass rates with suspicion — including the ones above. When the proficiency test was redesigned in 2006–08, Modern Standard Arabic pass rates collapsed to about 14%, and Persian-Farsi fell from roughly 92% to 50% — on the same instruction. In February 2009 a memo lowered the mastery cut score, and the very next class went from 34.5% meeting the standard to 92%. A 2.5× swing in “pass rate” was produced by an administrative signature. Any programme’s advertised numbers, including a language app’s, are partly a policy variable.

The dosage, honestly

A Category IV course — Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean — is 64 weeks at six classroom hours a day: roughly 1,920 contact hours, plus homework, with students pre-selected by aptitude test and studying as their full-time job.

At 30 minutes a day, 365 days a year, you accumulate 182 hours a year. The same hour count would take about ten and a half years — and that assumes perfect linearity, which nobody believes.

What that buys, and what it doesn't

Those six hours a day are not mostly buying vocabulary. They buy processing automaticity under time pressure and thousands of hours of interactive output with someone correcting it. That is the part 30 minutes a day cannot replicate.

What does transfer — and where the distributed learner genuinely has the advantage — is spacing. In a nine-year study, 13 sessions at 56-day intervals matched the retention of 26 sessions at 14-day intervals. Half the work, same result, for the memorisation layer specifically.

Three corrections your ten READMEs need. First, there is no Category V. Your Arabic, Chinese and Japanese READMEs all cite “FSI Category V” as a planning fact; the scale stops at IV, and all three languages are in it. Second, the hour figures moved: the old numbers were weeks × 25, the current ones are weeks × 23, and nearly every chart online still reproduces the obsolete set. Third, even the official Category IV figure is stale — 88 weeks × 23 is 2,024, but the page still says 2,200.

Use all of these as an ordinal ranking and nothing more — which is the conclusion How To Learn Languages.md already reached independently. They are class hours only; the same courses expect a further 17 hours a week of self-study, and they target level 3, a full step above what the military course aims at.

05 — Armory

Free, official, and better than most paid material

All of it is US government work, all of it is free, and most of it is built for exactly the skills in the library above. The reading and listening lessons are graded by the same ILR levels on the ladder, which means you can aim at a rung rather than at a vibe.
