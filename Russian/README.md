# Russian

**The book:** *The New Penguin Russian Course* — Nicholas J. Brown.
The long-standing self-study default: complete, grammar-honest, written for learners
without a classroom. Finish it with understanding and you hold a genuine A2-B1 base.
Its weakness is audio — pair with graded listening and Anki from day one.

**Free and legal alternative:** FSI Russian FAST course (US government, public domain)
— https://www.livelingua.com

**First move:** Cyrillic drilled to instant reading (2-3 days), then Brown ch. 1 and
the first 20 production cards (English → Russian, not recognition).

## Path to fluency (learn everything here → B2/C1)

1. **Foundation:** Brown, cover to cover, every exercise (→ A2-B1 grammar).
2. **Drill layer:** *Schaum's Outline of Russian Grammar* (Levine) for case/verb
   drills; FSI Russian audio for pronunciation patterning.
3. **Input engine (daily, from ch. 5 of Brown onward):**
   - Russian with Max — comprehensible-input podcast/YouTube: https://russianwithmax.com
   - Easy Russian (street interviews, dual subtitles): https://www.youtube.com/@EasyRussianVideos
   - Graded reading: *Short Stories in Russian* (Olly Richards), then Chekhov short
     stories with a dictionary.
4. **Speaking (the fluency maker):** 1-2 tutor sessions/week on https://www.italki.com
   from the moment Brown is half done. Production cards daily.
5. **Native transition:** Russian YouTube/films with Russian subtitles; the
   literature shelf (Dostoevsky in the original is this folder's summit).

**Milestone that means "fluent":** TORFL-II (B2) exam tasks feel doable; you can hold
a 30-minute unscripted conversation with a native speaker.
